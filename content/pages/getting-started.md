# Getting Started

Before you change how you study, it helps to see clearly how you study *now*.
Answer honestly — this is for you, not a grade. For each statement, note whether
it's **mostly true** or **mostly false** for you.

## Self-assessment

**A. How you take information in**
1. When I study, I mostly reread notes/textbook or re-watch lectures.
2. I try to make studying *easier* and lower-effort wherever I can.
3. I take notes by capturing as much as possible, close to word-for-word.

**B. How you organize knowledge**
4. I mostly learn facts and definitions on their own, as I meet them.
5. I rarely stop to ask how a new idea connects to what I already know.
6. My notes run left-to-right, top-to-bottom down the page (linear).

**C. How you test & revise**
7. I "test" myself by reading a question and checking if the answer looks right.
8. I rely heavily on flashcards / spaced repetition as my main method.
9. I revise one topic fully before moving to the next.

**D. How you think & stay consistent**
10. I focus on memorizing facts more than on judging what matters and why.
11. I only study when I feel motivated.

## What your answers mean

- **Mostly TRUE on A:** You're leaning on passive, low-effort intake. Biggest win
  first. → **[Myths & the Misinterpreted-Effort Trap](myths-mindset.html)** and
  **[The Effort–Time Exchange](effort-struggle.html)**.

- **Mostly TRUE on B:** Your knowledge is *isolated*, not integrated. → **[How
  Learning Really Works](the-science.html)**, then **[Encoding](encoding.html)**
  and **[Thinking on Paper](notes-on-paper.html)**.

- **Mostly TRUE on C:** Your retrieval and revision need an upgrade. → **[Active
  Recall, Done Right](active-recall.html)**, **[Spacing](spacing.html)**, and
  **[Interleaving](interleaving.html)**.

- **Mostly TRUE on D:** You're thinking at low levels and depending on motivation.
  → **[The 6 Levels of Thinking](higher-order-thinking.html)** and **[Motivation &
  Your Study System](motivation-systems.html)**.

## Where almost everyone should begin

Whatever your score, the most reliable order is simply to start at the top of the
**[Learning Materials](learning-materials.html)** and work down. The first three
lessons reset your foundations and mindset; everything else builds on them.

One commitment to make now: decide *how many focused minutes a day* you can
realistically protect. Even **15** is enough to start — you'll build your routine
around it in the [Study System](motivation-systems.html) lesson.
