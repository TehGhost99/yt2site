# Part 4 · Interleaving

> From Justin Sung's [interleaving for revision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XeKMjwiF3Pc).

After 10,000+ hours of studying — flashcards, active recall, practice questions,
brain dumps — interleaving stands out as one of the most powerful *revision*
techniques. ("Revision" = reviewing material you've already studied.)

## What it is

The intuitive way to revise is **blocking**: do all of topic A, then all of topic
B, then all of C. It feels smooth and you seem to improve fast within a block.

**Interleaving** mixes them up: A, C, B, A, B, C… Think of a Taekwondo drill
where the pad is held at a different distance each kick, so you must adjust every
time — versus kicking the identical target a hundred times. The mixed version is
harder, but it trains a skill the repetitive version never does.

## Why it works

When you block, you already know which method every problem needs, so you run on
autopilot. When you interleave, you must first **decide which approach a problem
calls for** — and *that* is exactly the skill exams and real life test. It also
improves memory, boosts test performance, and helps you handle concepts you
haven't seen before. As a bonus, mixing naturally spaces your practice.

## Four rules for doing it right

1. **Learn the basics first.** Interleaving is a *revision* tool. You need a
   foundational understanding of each topic before mixing them, or you're just
   confused.
2. **Mix related things.** Interleave topics/problem types that genuinely could be
   confused or combined, not random unrelated subjects — the value is in learning
   to *tell them apart* and choose between them.
3. **Switch before you're "done."** Don't finish a topic to completion; rotate
   while there's still a little struggle. The switching is the point.
4. **Expect it to feel worse.** Like every effective method here, interleaving
   feels harder and messier than blocking. That discomfort is the
   [desirable difficulty](myths-mindset.html) doing its job.

## How to use it

Once you've encoded a few related topics, build mixed review sets: alternate
problem types, shuffle subjects within a session, and mix the kinds of questions
you ask yourself. Pair it with [active recall](active-recall.html) at
[higher-order levels](higher-order-thinking.html) for the strongest effect.
