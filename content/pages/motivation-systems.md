# Part 5 · Motivation & Your Study System

> From Justin Sung's [DEFUSE method](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RMB3NsQotvM),
> his [guided study session](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JilEcEcojR4), and
> [15 lessons from 10,000 hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goHaIfMcfWo).

## Stop depending on motivation

Most people are **motivation-dependent**: they wait to *feel* like working, then
spend motivation overcoming every barrier. But motivation always fluctuates
(sleep, mood, health, who's around you), so productivity does too — and constantly
burning willpower leads to burnout. The goal is to be **motivation-enhanced**:
able to use motivation when you have it, but not *needing* it.

## The DEFUSE method

This builds *thought–action defusion* — separating what you do from how you feel:

- **D — Distinguish** feelings, thoughts, and actions. "I feel tired" is just a
  sensation; it doesn't have to trigger "therefore I must rest."
- **F — Fake it.** Like an actor playing a non-tired character, do the actions of
  someone who isn't tired. Bonus: acting productive makes you *feel* more
  productive (it works both ways). "Just train tired."
- **U — Uptime.** Gradually extend how long you can hold this state — 10 minutes
  today, 12 tomorrow, then 20, then 30. Neuroplasticity makes it easier over time.
- **Z — Zone.** Engineer a distraction-free environment. Keep a **distraction
  cheat sheet**: note every distraction as it happens, then later remove those
  triggers (app blockers, phone away, dedicated workspace).

## A structured study session

A focused session has phases — don't just dive in:

1. **Prep.** Optimize your environment: clear workspace, drinks, phone and
   computer on Do Not Disturb, tell people not to disturb you. Pre-eliminate any
   distraction you can predict.
2. **Encode.** Read actively and build the big picture in a non-linear map
   (see [Encoding](encoding.html) and [Thinking on Paper](notes-on-paper.html)).
   Embrace the early confusion.
3. **Consolidate / retrieve.** Use [active recall](active-recall.html) and, when
   needed, a small set of [spaced](spacing.html) flashcards for pure facts.
4. **Review later.** Revisit at spaced intervals, ideally
   [interleaved](interleaving.html).

## Hard-won lessons that tie it together

A few of the biggest from 10,000 hours of studying:

- **Cram early, not late.** If you cram well, cram the *whole* syllabus at the
  *start* of the term — then spend the remaining months finding and filling gaps
  in a relaxed way, instead of gambling everything on the night before.
- **Sleep is non-negotiable.** Justin got into med school studying 20 hours a day
  — and had a breakdown. Then the workload *doubled*. Working harder doesn't
  scale; learning *better* does.
- **Effort feels bad and is good.** The misinterpreted-effort trap will whisper
  that the hard methods aren't working. They are.
- **Improve by making mistakes faster.** The fastest learners try, fail, and
  adjust every hour or two. Don't wait for the perfect method.

## Where to go from here

Start small: protect a realistic daily block (even 15 focused minutes), pick
**one** technique — for most people, [active recall](active-recall.html) or
building the [big picture](encoding.html) — and run it for a week. Add the next
once it's a habit. Consistency, powered by good process, beats heroics.
