# Part 3 · The PACER Method

> From Justin Sung's [system for remembering everything you read](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okHkUIW46ks).

## Consume less, digest more

Reading has two stages: **consumption** (taking information in) and **digestion**
(processing it so it stays). Most people obsess over consuming more — reading
faster, lectures at 3×, binge-watching — but learning is "much less about what
goes into your brain and much more about what stays." Up to **90% of what you
read is forgotten** when you never digest it.

> **The balance rule:** everything you consume must be digested. If you don't
> have time to digest, *slow down and consume less.* Over-consuming without
> digesting is the learning equivalent of overeating — you just "vomit it back up"
> through forgetting.

## The five types of information

As you read, identify which category each piece belongs to. Each has a targeted
digestion process — using the wrong one wastes time.

| Letter | Type | What it is | Digest it by… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | **Procedural** | How to *do* something (techniques, coding, languages) | **Practice** — apply it in real life as early as possible |
| **A** | **Analogous** | Relates to something you already know | **Critique the analogy** — where does it hold, where does it break, can it be improved? |
| **C** | **Conceptual** | The *what*: facts, theories, relationships (most science) | **Map it** — non-linear, network-based notes (mind mapping) |
| **E** | **Evidence** | Specific facts/stats/cases that prove a concept | **Store & rehearse** — note it now, later practice *using* it |
| **R** | **Reference** | Nitty-gritty details you'll just need to recall | **Store & rehearse** — flashcards with spacing/active recall |

## The two biggest mistakes

1. **Trying to memorize on the spot.** For Procedural, Evidence, and Reference
   info, *don't* sit there rereading and forcing it into your head while you
   consume. Practice procedural info later; store evidence/reference info now and
   rehearse it later. Wasting consumption time on memorization steals time from
   the foundation (P, A, C).
2. **Treating everything as Reference.** Dumping everything onto flashcards is the
   "worst offender." Reference is the *easiest, least important* category — most
   of your real knowledge is Procedural, Analogous, and Conceptual.

## Why "forced" is the point

This system feels unnatural at first — and that's *why* it works. The brain has
real biological limits on how much it can take in at once, and in the modern
world it's easy to blow past them and get overwhelmed. Deliberately sorting and
digesting keeps you under that limit.

## Note the overlaps

Analogous info can live *inside* Procedural or Conceptual info — you can build an
analogy on a process or a concept to make it click. So as you map conceptual
material, look for analogies to add.
