## Research references

**Memory & cognitive architecture**

- Ebbinghaus, H. (1885). *Über das Gedächtnis.* — the original forgetting curve.
- Atkinson, R. C., & Shiffrin, R. M. (1968). Human memory: A proposed system and its control processes. *Psychology of Learning and Motivation, 2,* 89–195.
- Bartlett, F. C. (1932/1995). *Remembering.* Cambridge University Press. — schema theory.

**Cognitive Load Theory**

- Sweller, J. (2011). Cognitive load theory. *Psychology of Learning and Motivation, 55,* 37–76.
- Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (2019). Cognitive architecture and instructional design: 20 years later. *Educational Psychology Review, 31*(2), 261–292.
- Geary, D. C. (2008). An evolutionarily informed education science. *Educational Psychologist, 43*(4), 179–195.

**Misinterpreted effort & illusions of competence**

- Kirk-Johnson, A., Galla, B. M., & Fraundorf, S. H. (2019). Perceiving effort as poor learning: The misinterpreted-effort hypothesis. *Cognitive Psychology, 115,* 101237.
- Koriat, A., & Bjork, R. A. (2005). Illusions of competence in monitoring one's knowledge during study. *JEP: LMC, 31*(2), 187–194.
- Bjork, R. A., Dunlosky, J., & Kornell, N. (2013). Self-regulated learning: Beliefs, techniques, and illusions. *Annual Review of Psychology, 64,* 417–444.

**Retrieval practice (the testing effect)**

- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). The power of testing memory. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 1*(3), 181–210.
- Karpicke, J. D., & Roediger, H. L. (2008). The critical importance of retrieval for learning. *Science, 319*(5865), 966–968.
- Karpicke, J. D., Butler, A. C., & Roediger, H. L. (2009). Do students practise retrieval when they study on their own? *Memory, 17*(4), 471–479.
- Rowland, C. A. (2014). The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: A meta-analytic review. *Psychological Bulletin, 140*(6), 1432.
- Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017). Rethinking the use of tests: A meta-analysis of practice testing. *Review of Educational Research, 87*(3), 659–701.

**Spacing & the forgetting curve**

- Cepeda, N. J., et al. (2009). Optimizing distributed practice. *Experimental Psychology, 56*(4), 236–246.
- Latimier, A., Peyre, H., & Ramus, F. (2021). A meta-analytic review of the benefit of spacing out retrieval practice. *Educational Psychology Review, 33*(3), 959–987.
- Smith, C. D., & Scarf, D. (2017). Spacing repetitions over long timescales. *Frontiers in Psychology, 8,* 962.
- Bahrick, H. P., et al. (1993). Maintenance of foreign language vocabulary and the spacing effect. *Psychological Science, 4*(5), 316–321.

**Interleaving & flashcards**

- Wissman, K. T., Rawson, K. A., & Pyc, M. A. (2012). How and when do students use flashcards? *Memory, 20*(6), 568–579.
- Taylor, K., & Rohrer, D. (2010). The effects of interleaved practice. *Applied Cognitive Psychology, 24*(6), 837–848.
- Birnbaum, M. S., Kornell, N., Bjork, E. L., & Bjork, R. A. (2013). Why interleaving enhances inductive learning. *Memory & Cognition, 41*(3), 392–402.

**Higher-order thinking (Bloom's taxonomy)**

- Conklin, J. (2005). Review of the Anderson & Krathwohl revision of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Zaidi, N. L. B., et al. (2018). Pushing critical thinking skills with multiple-choice questions. *Academic Medicine, 93*(6).
- Lewis, A., & Smith, D. (1993). Defining higher order thinking. *Theory Into Practice, 32*(3), 131–137.
- Afflerbach, P., Cho, B.-Y., & Kim, J.-Y. (2015). Conceptualizing and assessing higher-order thinking in reading. *Theory Into Practice, 54*(3), 203–212.

**Note-taking & generative learning**

- Wittrock, M. C. (1989). Generative processes of comprehension. *Educational Psychologist, 24*(4), 345–376.
- Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. A. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. *Educational Research Review, 22,* 223–233.
- Mueller, P. A., & Oppenheimer, D. M. (2014). The pen is mightier than the keyboard. *Psychological Science, 25*(6), 1159–1168.
- Morehead, K., Dunlosky, J., & Rawson, K. A. (2019). How much mightier is the pen than the keyboard? *Educational Psychology Review, 31*(3), 753–780.
- Kiewra, K. A. (1989). A review of note-taking: The encoding-storage paradigm and beyond. *Educational Psychology Review, 1*(2), 147–172.

**Worked examples**

- Sweller, J., & Cooper, G. A. (1985). The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra. *Cognition and Instruction, 2*(1), 59–89.
- Renkl, A. (2014). Toward an instructionally oriented theory of example-based learning. *Cognitive Science, 38*(1), 1–37.

**Neuroplasticity**

- Sagi, Y., et al. (2012). Learning in the fast lane: New insights into neuroplasticity. *Neuron, 73*(6), 1195–1203.
- Bruel-Jungerman, E., Davis, S., & Laroche, S. (2007). Brain plasticity mechanisms and memory: A party of four. *The Neuroscientist, 13*(5), 492–505.

**Expertise, chunking & schema**

- Ericsson, K. A., & Charness, N. (1994). Expert performance: Its structure and acquisition. *American Psychologist, 49*(8), 725.
- Gobet, F., et al. (2001). Chunking mechanisms in human learning. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 5*(6), 236–243.
- Chiesi, H. L., Spilich, G. J., & Voss, J. F. (1979). Acquisition of domain-related information in relation to high and low domain knowledge. *JVLVB, 18*(3), 257–273.

**Metacognition & self-regulated learning**

- Dunning, D. (2011). The Dunning–Kruger effect. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 44,* 247–296.
- McDaniel, M. A., & Einstein, G. O. (2020). Training learning strategies to promote self-regulation and transfer. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 15*(6), 1363–1381.
- Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow.* Macmillan.
